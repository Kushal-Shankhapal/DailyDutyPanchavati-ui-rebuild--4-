📖 Official Docs: Tips and Tricks

• Figma AI Documentation: Lean on native Auto Layout settings before triggering AI canvas changes. If your structural frames lack rigid Auto Layout tags, generative features may deliver overlapping layers.
• Vercel v0 Best Practices: Use explicit component references like shadcn/ui in your system instructions. Avoid describing broad visual abstractions; instead, explicitly state functional items (e.g., "Generate a three-column dashboard layout with collapsible navigation tabs").

💡 Pro Hacks for October 2026

1. https://youtu.be/dWEvzEkg9hM?si=FjrXeZCqv5Op_VX4
This video from *UI Collective* explores the **honest reality of integrating AI into design systems**, debunking common myths and providing a practical workflow for designers. The host argues that while AI can assist in certain tasks, relying on automated solutions like *Design.md* files for complex design systems is often ineffective.

### **Key Takeaways:**
* **Why *Design.md* files fall short:** These files are often too thin to handle the complexity of enterprise-level design systems, and long files can confuse AI, leading to inaccurate results (0:39 - 4:00).
* **The limits of AI-generated components:** While AI can build basic elements like buttons, it struggles with complex components that require specific variants, properties, and correct nesting (6:21 - 9:30).
* **Where AI actually helps:**
    * **Layout Inspiration:** Using tools like *Mobbin* to research UI elements and then using AI to generate conceptual layouts (10:16 - 12:56).
    * **Typography:** Generating type scales is a well-suited task for AI, provided you set specific parameters like multiples of four (16:18 - 18:29).

### **Recommended AI Workflow:**
Instead of a single documentation file, the host suggests building **targeted skills** for AI to reference, keeping the system modular and manageable:
1. **Rules & Technical Debt:** Clearly define usage guidelines and known issues that cannot be fixed (19:47 - 22:24).
2. **Design Tokens/Variables:** Create a structured inventory that includes descriptions of when to use specific variables (22:24 - 25:34).
3. **Components:** Group components into logical categories—*Form Elements*, *Navigation*, and *Data Display*—to prevent AI from getting lost in the file structure (25:34 - 29:54).

Finally, the video emphasizes the importance of **auditing your designs** for "drift" to ensure they remain connected to your source design system (34:12 - 35:45).

2. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1NTKwpAVcHg
In this video, the creator condenses 10 years of web design experience into three core principles: **layout**, **color**, and **typography**. He emphasizes that building effective websites is less about trends and more about functionality and user experience.

### Core Design Pillars
* **Layout (1:19 - 2:20):** Move away from the outdated "F-pattern" and focus on **visual hierarchy**. Use sizing and contrast to guide users to the most important elements, like clear, high-contrast buttons. Avoid "ghost buttons" that are difficult for users to identify.
* **Color (2:20 - 3:47):** Ensure your palette is accessible and readable. Follow the **60-30-10 rule** (60% dominant color, 30% brand color, 10% accent color) to keep designs balanced and clean.
* **Typography (3:47 - 4:42):** Use hierarchy in your text (H1 for main headers, H2 for subheadings, and simple paragraph text) to ensure readability and scannability.

### Conversion and Growth
* **Design for Results (4:42 - 6:00):** A pretty website is useless if it doesn't convert. Focus on **clarity**, **scannability**, and **motivation** to help users achieve their goals. Always design for the target audience rather than personal preference.
* **Continuous Learning (6:23 - 7:03):** The industry changes rapidly. To stay relevant and compete with tools like AI, designers must commit to constant education and skill adaptation.
